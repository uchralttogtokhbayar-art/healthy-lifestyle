Key concept

So what IS a healthy lifestyle?

Health · WHO Constitution, 1948

“A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.”

Healthy lifestyle · WHO Europe, 1999

A way of living that lowers the risk of serious illness and early death — and lets people enjoy more of life.

In plain words: the daily choices — how you eat, move, sleep, cope and connect — that add up to the person you become.

Key point: health is not just "not being sick". It has three dimensions: physical, mental, social. A lifestyle is a pattern of behaviour, not one decision.

Why it matters

75%

of non-pandemic deaths worldwide in 2021 came from noncommunicable diseases: 43 million people. 18 million of them died before age 70.

Source: WHO NCD fact sheet, 2025

Four behaviours drive the risk

Tobacco use

Unhealthy diet

Harmful use of alcohol

Physical inactivity

All four are lifestyle factors — they can be changed.

NCDs = heart disease, cancer, diabetes, chronic lung disease. They are the world's main killers, and lifestyle is the main modifiable risk.

The rainbow model: our habits sit inside wider layers. Income, housing, work, culture and community shape what is easy or hard to choose. Link to human development: Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems make the same point.

01 / Move

Sit less. Move more.

The WHO minimum for adults: 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus strength work twice a week.

That is just 22 minutes a day. A brisk walk counts.

150 minutes sounds like a lot. Divide by seven: about 22 minutes a day.

02 / Eat

VEG + FRUIT

GRAINS

PROTEIN

Build the plate, skip the diet.

■ Half the plate: vegetables and fruit — aim for 5 portions a day.

■ Water first. Sugary drinks last.

■ Cook more. Ultra-processed less.

Use the plate, not a calorie app. Half veg and fruit, a quarter whole grains, a quarter protein.

03 / Sleep

7–9

Hours. Non-negotiable.

Same wake-up time, every day

Screens off 60 min before bed

Cool, dark, quiet room

Adults need 7 to 9 hours. Consistency of wake-up time matters more than bedtime.

04 / Mind

Stress is a signal. Not a lifestyle.

01 · Breathe slowly for 2 minutes

02 · Get outside, daylight, 20 minutes

03 · Talk to a real human, not a feed

Mental health is a pillar, not an extra. People and nature are the cheapest medicine.

A new life stage

Arnett (2000) named 18–25 “emerging adulthood”: a time of exploration and instability.

First real control

For many, the first years of choosing your own food, sleep, money and substances.

Habits that stick

Patterns set in this window often carry into adulthood — a key time to intervene (Nelson et al., 2008).

Ask: who here cooks for themselves now? Who sleeps less than at school? University is exactly the stage where lifestyle gets "locked in".

Theory · Prochaska & DiClemente (1983)

Change happens in stages

Precontemplation

“I don't see a problem.”

Contemplation

“Maybe I should…”

Preparation

“I start next Monday.” A concrete plan.

Action

Doing it, for less than 6 months.

Maintenance

6+ months. It's now part of who you are.

The Transtheoretical Model. People move up and down these steps; relapse is normal, not failure. Different stages need different help: information early, planning in the middle, support at the end.

66

DAYS

Research · Lally et al. (2010)

Habits take longer than 21 days

The median time for a new daily behaviour to feel automatic — with a range of 18 to 254 days.

Missing a single day did not ruin the process.

Consistency beats intensity.

The "21 days" idea is a myth. 96 participants built habits like drinking water or a short run; it took a median of 66 days. Be patient with yourself.

Discussion · 5 min in pairs

Healthy lifestyle: personal choice or social privilege?

Choice

Walking, sleep and water cost nothing. Daily decisions are ours to make.

Privilege

Income, food prices, work hours, air quality and safe streets limit what is possible.
